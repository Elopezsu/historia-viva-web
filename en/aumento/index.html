A 3-step script · with a true story

Before you ask for a raise, read this

It happens to so many of us: we ask for the raise scared, accept the first “no” and walk out with nothing. A hundred years ago, about 400 factory workers asked for higher pay without a cent saved. I'll tell you what they did and how to use it today.

The story

A hundred years ago, in a textile mill in Bello, next to Medellín, about 400 women workers worked from six in the morning to six in the evening. They went barefoot. They were charged fines that came out of their pay. And they earned far less than the men for the same work.

One day they stopped going in. One of them, Betsabé Espinal, spoke for all of them and read out their list of demands. A journalist asked one of the strikers what savings they had to fall back on. She answered: «Con ninguno. Solo nuestro carácter» (“None. Only our character”).

Three weeks later, the mill gave in. They won a 40 % raise, fewer working hours and the removal of the bosses who harassed them.

  1. STEP 1

    What to say: a concrete request, not venting

    Before the meeting, write down three things you achieved this year and the exact number you're going to ask for. Walk in with that on paper.

    • “I'd like to talk about my salary. This year I achieved [1], [2] and [3]. That's why I'm asking to go from [current amount] to [new amount]. How can we make it work?”

    What they did: they didn't just go to complain. They brought a short list and read it out loud.

  2. STEP 2

    If they tell you “there's no budget”

    Don't argue about the budget. Ask about the path to having one.

    • “I understand. What would need to happen for there to be one?”
    • “When is the budget reviewed? I'd like my case to be part of that review.”
    • “What results do you want to see from me by then?”

    What they did: they had no savings and still held their demand for three weeks. The “no” on day one wasn't the end of the conversation.

  3. STEP 3

    If they say no: ask for something else, in writing

    Salary isn't the only thing you can negotiate. You can ask for a date to review it, flexible hours, a paid course, a change of title or days off.

    • “If it's not possible today, can we put in writing a date to review it and what I need to achieve?”
    • On your way out, an email: “Thank you for the conversation. Here's a summary of what we agreed: …”

    What they did: besides pay, they asked to wear shoes, fewer hours and an hour for lunch. And when it was announced that everything had been settled, they decided to stay on strike until they had an agreement signed by both sides.

One honest thing

No phrase guarantees a yes. This script helps you walk in with a clear request and not walk out empty-handed.

They had no savings.

They had a list. And they didn't let it go.
