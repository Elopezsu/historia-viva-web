The decision you keep putting off

The one you've been thinking about for months. Changing jobs, launching your own thing, leaving, staying, saying yes. I'm not going to tell you what to decide. I'm giving you seven short questions, one a day, so you stop carrying it without looking at it.

Before you start, a story

Maria Augusta decided before they let her

Almost a hundred and fifty years ago, in Rio de Janeiro, a teenage girl read in a newspaper that a woman in the United States had graduated as a doctor. In Brazil, women couldn't go to university.

She told her father she wanted to study medicine. He asked her not to worry so much about the future: medicine wasn't for women. She had already made up her mind, and she convinced him with her arguments. At fifteen, she boarded a ship to New York. First she took a preparatory course; then she entered a medical school for women.

When the family ran out of money, the emperor gave her a scholarship. Her father died before he could see her graduate. She finished. Her name was Maria Augusta Generoso Estrela, and she was the first Brazilian woman with a medical degree. She came back, took the exams again in Rio to get her diploma recognized, and worked there for many years.

Her father died before he could see her graduate. She finished: Maria Augusta Generoso Estrela was the first Brazilian woman with a medical degree.

What I love most about her story: Brazil opened its universities to women four years after she left. She didn't wait for the world to be ready. From New York, she wrote to her father:

"Never, not for a single moment, have I regretted embracing this profession: the more I study, the more I want to learn."Letter from Maria Augusta to her father, quoted by June Hahner (my translation)

How to use it: one question a day, five minutes, by hand if you can. Your answers don't have to be pretty. If you skip a day, just pick up with the next one.

  1. Day 1

    Name it

    Write the decision in a single sentence. Start with "I'm deciding whether…". How long have you been carrying it?

  2. Day 2

    What not deciding costs you

    Every week that goes by without deciding, what do you pay? Time, money, sleep, energy. Write down three things.

  3. Day 3

    The "no" you imagine

    Who would tell you no, and with what argument? Write it down. Underneath, your answer. She didn't wait for permission: she gave her reasons.

  4. Day 4

    The newspaper you read

    Who did you see do something similar and think "me too"? Write down their name and what they did.

  5. Day 5

    Your preparatory course

    She didn't go straight to medical school. What's the smallest step you could take in the next 48 hours?

  6. Day 6

    The women in your family

    What decisions couldn't your mother, your grandmother, your great-grandmother make? Studying, leaving, separating, managing their own money. One line for each. Then: which of those can you make?

    This isn't therapy. It's looking at your family through a historian's eyes. If something feels very heavy, stop and talk it over with someone you trust.

  7. Day 7

    Decide, or decide when

    Fill in one: "I decide ___" or "I'll decide on ___, and until then I'll ___". Date it and sign it. If you want, tell someone, the way she wrote to her father.

She had to fight to be able to choose. Today we have more open doors.

Let the decision be yours, not time's.
