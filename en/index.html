Sixteen years building stages for other people to speak on.

At 19 I moved alone from Venezuela to Colombia with what fit in one suitcase. I started at the bottom, in tech support.

Then came AT&T and Cisco, where I led a global e-learning program with 280 people. Later webinar.net, the FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup and ValuSignal, the first national virtual conference for US appraisers.

Along the way I traveled to 18 countries on my own, and in each one I looked for the same thing: the history that isn't in the brochures.

Now I'm the one on stage, talking about the one thing I never stopped studying.

Three talks. None of them need your people to like history.

Built from archives, with names, dates and consequences, and shaped to your date: Women's History Month, Hispanic Heritage Month, a wellbeing week or year-end.

For Women's History Month

The women who decided first.

Women of this continent who made a decision before us, with less and more against them. It works with mixed audiences, and works better.

For learning and wellbeing weeks

Who wrote what you believe.

Three questions that fit on a card and work just as well on a document from 1781 as on this morning's headline or an internal report.

For Hispanic Heritage Month and global teams

Where your team comes from.

The words, the surnames and what repeats from one generation to the next. The session people keep talking about in the chat.
